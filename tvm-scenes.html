Time Value of Money

A rupee today is not the same as a rupee tomorrow. Seven short scenes, each with something to play with. Pick a topic from the tabs, or work through them in order.

1 · Future value

The snowball

Your grandmother puts ₹1,00,000 in a deposit the day you are born and forgets about it. Two banks: one pays simple interest, the other compounds. Race them for 30 years.

Guess first: at 10%, what does the compounding deposit hold at age 30?
The idea
FV = PV × (1 + r)ⁿ

Compounding pays interest on interest, so growth speeds up every year. The flags show the money doubling roughly every 72 ÷ r years: the rule of 72.

3 · Future value of an annuity

The SIP jar

From your first job you drop ₹50,000 into a jar at the end of every year. Each year's money keeps growing for the years that remain. Watch the old layers swell.

The idea
FV = C × [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1] ÷ r

Each payment grows for a different number of years. The formula adds them all up in one step. The longer you save, the more of the jar is growth rather than your own money.

4 · Present value of an annuity

How big a loan can you take?

You can afford ₹2,40,000 a year (₹20,000 a month) in repayments. The bank charges 9%. The loan the bank will give you today is the value of those future payments, brought back to today.

The idea
PV = C × [1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ] ÷ r

Each repayment is worth less today the later it comes. The loan amount is the sum of those shrunken payments. Everything you repay above it is interest.

5 · Perpetuity

A scholarship forever

An alumna wants to fund a ₹1,00,000 scholarship every year, forever. How much must she give today? Each year's ₹1,00,000 is worth less today than the last. Count the years and watch them fill a bar that never overflows.

The idea
PV = C ÷ r

Far-off payments are worth almost nothing today, so the running total levels off. If she invests C ÷ r at rate r, the interest alone pays the scholarship every year and the gift is never used up.
